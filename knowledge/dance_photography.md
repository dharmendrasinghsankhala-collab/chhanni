# Dance photography (Bharatanatyam / Kathak)

## What makes a keeper
- Peak of the movement: the held pose at the end of an adavu / tihai, not the transition.
- Bharatanatyam: araimandi (half-sitting) with knees open and symmetric; straight spine; clean mudra with all fingers visible.
- Kathak: chakkar (spins) — skirt (lehenga/angarkha) fully flared; tatkar — feet and ghungroo sharp; sudden stops (sam) are peak moments.
- Abhinaya: eyes and eyebrows carry emotion — face sharp, eyes open, gaze direction clear.
- Hands and feet must not be cropped at joints; a cut-off hand in a mudra is a reject.
- Costume fan (pleats) open and visible in Bharatanatyam poses.

## Technique
- Freeze: 1/500 s+ for footwork/hands; 1/1000 s for chakkar.
- Motion feel: drag shutter 1–2 s with ND + flash/LED burst to freeze the face while fabric streaks; shorten if the face ghosts.
  ([Fstoppers, Nov 2026](https://fstoppers.com/editorial/how-i-shot-three-completely-different-portraits-profotos-essential-hybrid-kit-905308))
- Framing: tight for abhinaya, medium/wide for nritta and full-body mudras.
  ([Fstoppers – Platon](https://fstoppers.com/education/why-platon-wont-show-presidents-back-his-camera-905501))
- Stage light is usually warm + coloured; white balance on skin, not on the coloured wash.
- Shoot from slightly below stage level for lines; avoid shooting from far above (shortens the dancer).

## Culling rules (photo_cull)
- Reject: closed eyes in abhinaya, blurred face, cropped hands/feet, mid-transition limbs.
- Keep a little motion blur in hands/ghungroo if face is sharp — it shows energy.
- Prefer frames where the costume pleats/skirt are fully open.
