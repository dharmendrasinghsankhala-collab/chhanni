# Oran — Rajasthan ke pavitra van (project brief for the "Oran" documentary)

Researched Nov 2026. Used by Chhanni's AI Team when it plans / scripts the Oran documentary.

## What an oran is
- A community-protected sacred grove / grazing commons in Rajasthan, dedicated to a local deity (devi, pir, lok-devta).
  Other local names: dev-van, devbani, deorai, rakhat bani, mandir van, bani. Usually has a temple/than, and a water
  body: nadi (small pond), talab, johad, beri, agor (catchment).
- Rules are customary: no cutting green trees, no hunting, no polluting the water; grazing is allowed. Dead wood only.
- Typical flora: khejri (Prosopis cineraria, worshipped, gives sangri), ker (Capparis decidua), ber/jhadi, rohida,
  kumat, desi babool, jaal/pilu. Fauna: chinkara, blackbuck, nilgai, desert fox, jackal, caracal, peafowl, demoiselle
  cranes (kurja) in winter, and the critically endangered Great Indian Bustard (godavan) in Jaisalmer orans.
- Scale: commonly cited ~25,000 orans in Rajasthan; activist estimates ~5.8-6 lakh hectares, much of it recorded as
  "wasteland" (banjar / gair-mumkin) in revenue records after the 1950s land settlements.
- Large western orans: Degrai Mata (Jaisalmer, ~10,000 ha, ~600+ years, villages incl. Sanwata, Achla), Bhadariya
  Rai (Jaisalmer, 15-18 k ha), Dungar Pir ji (Mokla), Aala ji (Kuchdi), Tanot Rai. Eastern ones: Alwar (KRAPAVIS /
  Aman Singh), Jaipur, Jhunjhunu.
- Pastoral life: Raika/Rabari camel & sheep herders, Bhati Rajputs, Bishnoi, Meghwal, Gujjar (east) depend on orans.
  Women collect ker-sangri; red threads tied on khejri for rain / children / wishes; fairs (mela) and bhandaras keep
  the rules alive.
- Bishnoi memory: Khejarli 1730 — Amrita Devi and 363 people died hugging khejri trees. Still the moral reference.

## Timeline of the fight (for the "stakes" act)
- 2005: Supreme Court's Central Empowered Committee recommends orans be classed as forests.
- 2018: SC (Godavarman case) orders orans recorded as deemed forests — little compliance.
- 1 Feb 2024: Rajasthan notifies orans/dev-vans/rundhs as "deemed forest" (only those already recorded; ~4 lakh ha).
  Many villagers object, fearing loss of access to the forest department.
- 18 Dec 2024: SC (on Aman Singh / KRAPAVIS application) holds orans are "forest" under the Forest Conservation Act
  1980 regardless of revenue entry; orders ground + satellite mapping, community-reserve status, FRA protection, and
  asks MoEFCC for a national sacred-grove policy. Open question: who is custodian — community or forest dept.
- 2021 onward: Degrai oran — high-tension lines of solar/wind projects; studies record dozens of large-bird deaths;
  SC orders undergrounding lines in GIB habitat.
- 2023-25: ~44,000 ha allotted to solar parks (23 GW) in west Rajasthan; Rajasthan target 125 GW RE by 2030.
- 2025-26: Jaisalmer sit-ins (22-day, 34-day) win registration proposals for ~17,500 bigha oran + ponds/agor.
  21 Jan 2026: "Oran Bachao Padyatra" ~700-725 km from Tanot Mata to Jaipur, led by Sumer Singh Bhati (Team Oran,
  Sanwata); stopped by police in Jaipur 16 Apr 2026; same day govt announces ~3,666 ha reserved as oran in Jaisalmer.
  Slogan: "Oran bachao, gochar bachao". Feb 2026: Khejri Bachao Andolan (Bikaner) wins promise of a khejri law.
- Aug 2026: Sumer Singh Bhati among Rangeland Awards 2026 winners for Degrai Mata Oran restoration.
- Fairness note: the state and companies argue solar is national climate priority and much land is genuinely barren;
  villagers say "we are not against solar — keep it off the oran / use rooftops & small community plants". A good
  film lets both be heard.

## Language notes for interviews (Marwari / Thali / Rajasthani)
- Transcribe with Whisper language = Hindi; Marwari words will come out approximately — fix by context.
- oran / orann = sacred grove; gochar = village grazing land; agor = rain catchment of a pond; nadi = pond;
  beri = shallow well; tanka = covered cistern; khadin = runoff-farming bund; than / thaan = shrine;
  dhani = hamlet; bhopa = priest/folk-singer; mataji / devi-ji = goddess; godavan = Great Indian Bustard;
  kurja = demoiselle crane; sangri = khejri pods; ker = caper berry; bigha = land unit (~0.25 ha in Jaisalmer, varies);
  khasra = plot number; patwari = land-record official; banjar / gair-mumkin = "wasteland" entry; patta = lease.
- "Mhare", "mharo" = our; "bai-sa" / "hukum" = respectful address; "kai" = what; "ghano" = a lot.

## Documentary angles that fit the material
1. Faith as conservation law: the goddess is the forest guard ("devi ri jameen, koi kaat ni sake").
2. One day in the oran: dawn graziers → women & khejri → noon at the nadi → evening aarti/prasad → night sky.
3. Paper vs ground: the land that is "wasteland" on paper but full of life on the ground.
4. The new sun: solar plates and power lines at the edge of the oran; godavan and wires.
5. The march: ordinary herders walking 700 km — from Tanot to Jaipur.
Structure that usually works: cold open on a sacred image + one strong line → world → ritual & ecology →
threat → resistance → open question/hope; let elders carry the meaning, avoid narration where people can speak.
